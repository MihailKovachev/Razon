# The Substitution Theorem

>[!THEOREM] The Substitution Theorem
>
>Let $\mathcal{N}_1$ and $\mathcal{N_2}$ be two [networks](./Lumped%20Networks.md) which are connected through four [terminals](./Lumped%20Networks.md) $T_{\mathcal{N}_1,1} \leftrightarrow T_{\mathcal{N}_2,1}$ and $T_{\mathcal{N}_1,2} \leftrightarrow T_{\mathcal{N}_2,2}$ such that $T_{\mathcal{N}_1,1}$, $\leftrightarrow T_{\mathcal{N}_1,2}$ form a [port](./Ports.md) and $T_{\mathcal{N}_2,1}$, $\leftrightarrow T_{\mathcal{N}_2,2}$ also form a [port](./Ports.md):
>
>![Substitution Theorem](./res/Substitution%20Theorem.svg)
>
>If it is possible to determine $v(t)$ solely from $\mathcal{N}_2$ and $\mathcal{N}_1$ is [voltage-controlled](./Lumped%20Networks.md), then $\mathcal{N}_2$ is equivalent to an [ideal voltage source](../Analog%20Circuits/Sources.md#Ideal%20Voltage%20Sources):
>
>![Substitution Theorem Voltage Source](./res/Substitution%20Theorem%20Voltage%20Source.svg)
>
>If it is possible to determine $i(t)$ solely from $\mathcal{N}_2$ and $\mathcal{N}_1$ is [current-controlled](./Lumped%20Networks.md), then $\mathcal{N}_2$ is equivalent to an [ideal current source](../Analog%20Circuits/Sources.md#Ideal%20Current%20Sources):
>
>![Substitution Theorem Current Source](./res/Substitution%20Theorem%20Current%20Source.svg)
