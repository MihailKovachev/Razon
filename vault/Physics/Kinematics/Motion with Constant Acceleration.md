# Motion with Constant Acceleration

>[!THEOREM] Theorem: Velocity under Constant Acceleration
>
>If a [point particle](../Classical%20Mechanics/Point%20Particles.md) is moving with constant [acceleration](./Acceleration.md) $\boldsymbol{a}(t) = \boldsymbol{a}$ between two moments $t_0$ and $t \ge t_0$, then its [velocity](./Velocity.md) at $t$ is
>
>$$\boldsymbol{v}(t) = \boldsymbol{v}_0 + (t - t_0)\boldsymbol{a},$$
>
>where $\boldsymbol{v}_0 = \boldsymbol{v}(t_0)$.

>[!THEOREM] Theorem: Position under Constant Acceleration
>
>If a [point particle](../Classical%20Mechanics/Point%20Particles.md) is moving with constant [acceleration](./Acceleration.md) $\boldsymbol{a}(t) = \boldsymbol{a}$ between two moments $t_0$ and $t \ge t_0$, then its [position](./Position.md) at $t$ is
>
>$$\boldsymbol{r}(t) = \boldsymbol{r}_0 + (t-t_0)\boldsymbol{v}_0 + \frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{a}(t-t_0)^2,$$
>
>where $\boldsymbol{v}_0 = \boldsymbol{v}(t_0)$ and $\boldsymbol{r}_0 = \boldsymbol{r}(t_0)$ are its [velocity](./Velocity.md) and [position](./Position.md) at $t_0$, respectively.

>[!THEOREM] Theorem: Torricelli's Formula
>
>If a [point particle](../Classical%20Mechanics/Point%20Particles.md) is moving with constant [acceleration](./Acceleration.md) $\boldsymbol{a}(t) = \boldsymbol{a}$ between two moments $t_0$ and $t \ge t_0$, then its [speed](./Velocity.md) at $t$ is related to its initial [speed](./Velocity.md) and [displacement](./Displacement.md) by
>
>$$||\boldsymbol{v}(t)||^2 = ||\boldsymbol{v}_0||^2 + 2\boldsymbol{a} \cdot (\boldsymbol{r}(t) - \boldsymbol{r}_0),$$
