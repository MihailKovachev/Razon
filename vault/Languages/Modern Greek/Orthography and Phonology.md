Sometimes, the letter combinations *αι*, *ει*, *οι*, *υι*, *αυ*, *ευ*, *ηυ*, *ου* are not meant to be treated as digraphs and their letters are supposed to be pronounced separately. This is indicated by placing a diaeresis (`¨`) on the second letter. If the [stress](#Accent) falls on the first letter of such a combination, then the [acute accent](#Accent) is written on it and the diaeresis (`¨`) is omitted (e.g. "τσάι" and not "τσάϊ").

## Accent Marks

The acute accent `´` is used to mark [accented](#Accent) syllables in polysyllabic words and is placed on the vowel of said syllables. If the vowel is an uppercase letter, then the accent is placed up and to the left of it. If the letter is lowercase, then the accent is placed on top of it. In [digraphs](#Digraphs), the second letter takes the accent. 

>[!EXAMPLE]- Example: Acute Accent for Stress
>
>είμαι /'i.me̞/
>
>αύριο /'av.ɾi.o/
>

The acute accent can also appear in monosyllabic words to distinguish between words which would otherwise be written in the same way.

>[!EXAMPLE]- Example: Acute Accent as Distinguisher
>
>πώς (how?, what?) <-> πως (that)
>
>ή (or) <-> η (the)
>

The diaeresis `¨` is used to indicate that two consecutive letters should *not* be treated as a [digraph](#Digraphs). It is placed on the second letter.

## Accent

Similarly to English, [Modern Greek](./index.md) has a stress accent system in which every word has a syllable pronounced slightly louder and longer than the others. This syllable is marked by an [acute accent](#Accent%20Marks).

>[!IMPORTANT] Important: The Antepenultimate Rule
>
>The stress of a word can only be in one of three possible positions:
>- **oxytone** - on the ultima (last syllable);
>- **paroxytone** - on the penult (second-to-last syllable);
>- **proparoxytone** - on the antepenult (third-to-last syllable).
>
>This rule is never broken. It is so strong that it can force the stress to move in inflected forms of the same word, lest it is broken. 
>

>[!INFO] Info: Derived Stress
>
>In certain situations, a single word may have two accented syllables:
>- If a [noun](./Nouns.md) / [adjective](./Adjectives.md) / [adverb](./Adverbs.md) / [verb](./Verbs.md) / [preposition](./Prepositions.md)  has a [proparoxytone accent](#Accent) and is followed by a [weak personal pronoun](./Pronouns.md#Personal%20Pronouns) or a [possessive pronoun](./Pronouns.md#Possessive%20Pronouns), then a second accent occurs on the ultima.
>
>>[!EXAMPLE]-
>>
>>ο γείτονάς μας [o ˈʝi.to.'nas mas] ("our neighbor")
>>
>>φύλαξέ το [ˈfi.laks.'e to] ("keep it")
>>
>>απέναντí μας [a.'pe.nand.'i mas] ("opposite us")
>>
>
>- If an [imperative verb](./Verbs.md) has a [paroxytone accent](#Accent) and is followed by two [weak personal pronouns](./Pronouns.md#Personal%20Pronouns), then the one closest to it also has an accent.
>
>>[!EXAMPLE]-
>>
>>δώσε τού το ['ðo.se 'tu to] ("give it to him")
>>
>>φέρε μού τα [ˈfe.re 'mu ta] ("bring them to me")
>>
>
>This secondary stress is also marked by an [acute accent](#Accent%20Marks).
>
