# Reference Directions

We can illustrate the [currents](../Current.md) flowing through a circuit and the [voltages](TODO) across various points inside it using the so called **reference arrows**. We are free to arbitrarily choose their direction, so long as we stick to our choice once we have made it. This is because the actual direction is determined by the combination of algebraic sign and reference direction:
- A positive [current](../Current.md) means that the direction in which [conventional current](../Current.md) flows is the same as the chosen reference direction. A negative [current](../Current.md) means that the direction in which [conventional current](../Current.md) flows is the opposite to the chosen reference direction.
- A positive [voltage](TODO) means that the reference arrow points from the point with a higher [potential](TODO) towards the point with a lower [potential](TODO). A negative [voltage](TODO) means that the reference arrow points from the point with a lower [potential](TODO) towards the point with a higher [potential](TODO).

![Reference Directions](./res/Reference%20Directions.svg)

Physically, these reference arrows correspond to the way [currents](../Current.md) and [voltages](TODO) are measured. [Ammeters](TODO) are calibrated to output positive numbers when the flow of [conventional current](../Current.md) is from their positive [terminal](./Network%20Analysis.md) to their negative [terminal](./Network%20Analysis.md) and to output negative numbers when [conventional current](../Current.md) flows in the opposite direction. Similarly, [voltmeters](TODO) are calibrated to output positive numbers when the [electric potential](TODO) is higher at their positive [terminal](./Network%20Analysis.md) and to output negative numbers when the [electric potential](TODO) is higher at their negative [terminal](./Network%20Analysis.md) .
