# Nouns (Bulgarian)

## Gender

Nouns in Bulgarian can take on one of three genders - masculine, feminine and neuter. It is not possible to determine the gender of noun solely by looking at it, but most nouns do exhibit certain patterns depending on their ending.

### Masculine Nouns

Most singular nouns ending in a consonant are masculine.

>[!EXAMPLE]- Example: Masculine Nouns Ending in a Consonant
>
>абона̀т (subscriber), бог (god), войн (warrior), дом (home), клас (class), кок (bun), кон (horse), край (end), о̀блак (cloud), плод (fruit), под (floor), пол (gender), поро̀й (deluge), син (son), слой (layer), стол (chair), това̀р (load), ток (electrical current), хляб (bread)
>

### Feminine Nouns

Most singular nouns ending in *-а* or *-я* are feminine.

>[!EXAMPLE]- Example: Feminine Nouns in *-а*
>
>бѝтка (fight), буболѐчка (bug), вода̀ (water), война̀ (war), звезда̀ (star), ко̀ла (coca cola), кола̀ (car), кру̀ша (pear), ку̀ла (tower), ма̀са (table; mass), омра̀за (hatred), су̀ша (drought), ча̀ша (cup), я̀бълка (apple), я̀года (strawberry)
>

>[!EXAMPLE]- Example: Feminine Nouns in *-я*
>
>ауто̀псия (autopsy), инфѐкция (infection), мизѐрия (misery), мръсотѝя (dirt), пикня̀ (piss), повърня̀ (vomit), помѝя, сѐрия (set, series), смръдня̀ (stench), чѐкия (handjob)
>

Many singular nouns ending in *-ст* and especially those which also represent abstract ideas are feminine.

>[!EXAMPLE]- Example: Feminine Singular Nouns Ending in *-ст*
>
>анонѝмност (anonymity), блѝзост (proximity), власт (control), въ̀зраст (age), дъ̀рзост (daringness), гото̀вност (readiness) мла̀дост (young age), мъст (vengeance), нена̀вист (hatred), пръст (dirt), ста̀рост (old age), съвършѐност (perfection)
>

### Neuter Nouns

Most singular nouns ending in *-е* or *-о* are neuter.

>[!EXAMPLE]- Example: Neuter Singular Nouns Ending in *-е*
>
>врѐме (time; weather), детѐ (child), морѐ (sea), небѐ (sky), плѐме (tribe), прасѐ (pig), сѐме (seed), шишѐ (bottle)
>

>[!EXAMPLE]- Example: Neuter Singular Nouns Ending in *-о*
>
>бута̀ло (piston), жѝло (stinger), колело̀ (wheel; bicycle), кормѝло (steering wheel), насеко̀мо (insect), нача̀ло (beginning), огледа̀ло (mirror), седло̀ (saddle)
>

All diminutives formed via the suffixes *-е*, *-ле*, *-че*, *-енце* are neuter.

>[!EXAMPLE]- Example: Neuter Diminutive Nouns Ending in *-е*, *-ле*, *-че*, *-енце*
>
>|Noun|Diminutive|
>|:--:|:--:|
>|брат (brother)|братлѐ (bro), братлѐнце (little bro), бра̀тче (little brother), бра̀тченце (little brother)|
>|бъ̀лгарин (Bulgarian)|бъ̀лгарче (little Bulgarian)|
>|гра̀жданим (citizen)|гра̀жданче (little citizen)|
>|козѐл (male goat)|козлѐ (little goat)|
>|сѐлянин (villager)|сѐлянче (little villager)|
>|сестра̀ (sister)|сестрѝче (little sister)|
>|учѝтел (teacher)|учѝтелче (little teacher)|
>

## Number

Nouns in Bulgarian can be either **singular** or **plural**. Unlike in English, however, building the plural form of a noun from its singular form is not as easy as just adding a letter or two. The ending for the plural form of a noun can depend on several factors such as the noun's singular ending, its gender and its number of syllables. 

### Plural Masculine Nouns

The vast majority of monosyllabic masculine nouns ending in a consonant form the plural by appending *-ове* to the singular. This is especially true of nouns of foreign origin, although exceptions exist even there.

- The [stress](./Phonology%20(Bulgarian).md#Stress) in the resulting form is generally unpredictable.
- One should beware changes in a [variable ya](./Yat%20Reflex%20(Bulgarian).md).

>|хляб (bread)|хля̀бове||

Most polysyllabic masculine nouns form the plural by appending *-и*.

- With very few exceptions, there is no [stress](./Phonology%20(Bulgarian).md#Stress) shift in the plural.
- When the singular ends in *-й*, *-а* or *-я*, then these are dropped from the plural.
- An [unstressed](./Phonology%20(Bulgarian).md#Stress) ⟨ъ⟩ in the ultima of the singular which is surrounded by an [obstruent](TODO) and [sonorant](TODO) is dropped in the plural. Most words ending in *-ък* are exceptions to this rule.
- If the ultima of the singular contains ⟨е⟩ originating from [Proto-Slavic](TODO) ⟨ь⟩, then this ⟨е⟩ is dropped in the plural.
- If the singular ends in the suffix *-ец* preceded by ⟨а⟩, ⟨ъ⟩, ⟨о⟩, ⟨у⟩, ⟨е⟩ or ⟨и⟩, then the ⟨е⟩ in the suffix changes to ⟨й⟩.
- In most cases, a final *-г*, *-к*, *-х* in the singular transitions into ⟨з⟩, ⟨ц⟩, ⟨с⟩, respectively, in the plural. This transition rarely takes place in nouns of foreign origin.
- If the singular ends in the suffix *-ин*, then this suffix is dropped in the plural.

#### Count Form

Most masculine nouns whose singular ends in a consonant also have a special plural form known as the **count form** (**бро̀йна фо̀рма**).

Most masculine nouns construct the [count form](#Count%20Form) by appending *-а*.

>[!INFO] Info: Count Forms of *метър* and *литър*
>
>When the words *мѐтър* аnd *лѝтър* signify units of measurement, then their [count forms](#Count%20Form) are *мѐтра* and *лѝтра*. In all other cases, their [count forms](#Count%20Form) are *мѐтъра* and *лѝтъра*.
>
>The [count forms](#Count%20Form) of their derivatives are formed accordingly.
>

##### Count Form Usage

The [count form](#Count%20Form) is only used with nouns which do *not* signify persons.

It is used when the noun is modified by an exact [cardinal numeral](./Numerals%20(Bulgarian).md#Cardinal%20Numerals) ("два", "сто и един", "хиляда и сто", etc.).

>[!EXAMPLE]-
>
>Би ли ми дал онези **два** **стола**? (Would you give me those two chairs?)
>
>**Четирите** **пътя** са свързани с кръгово. (The four roads are joined by a roundabout.)
>

The [count form](#Count%20Form) is used with [approximative numerals](./Numerals%20(Bulgarian).md) ("четири-пет", "седем-осем").

>[!EXAMPLE]-
>
>Остават **четири-пет** **дена**. (There four or five days remaining).
>

The [count form](#Count%20Form) is used with the [adverbs](./Adverbs%20(Bulgarian).md) "колко", "колкото", "толкова" and "няколко" when they signify a quantity in a non-explicative sentence.

>[!EXAMPLE]-
>
>Трябват ми още **няколко** **часа**, за да приключа с работата. (I need a few more hours to finish the work).
>
>**Колко лева** струва това? (How many levs does this cost?)
>
>**Колко** бели **коня** имаш? (How many white horses do you have?)
>

The [count form](#Count%20Form) is *not* used if "колко" or "толкова" are used to amplify something rather than to express a particular quantity. In these cases, the normal plural form is used.

>[!EXAMPLE]-
>
>**Колко** хубави **коне** имаш! (You have such beautiful horses!)
>
>Не очаквах чак **толкова** **проблеми**. (I did not expect so many problems.)
>

The [count form](#Count%20Form) is *not* used when the noun is modified by "колцѝна", "неко̀лцина" and "малцѝна". Instead, the normal plural form is used.

## Case

The case inflections for nouns have largely vanished, leaving only a general and a vocative singular case as well as a few minor remnants.

Most masculine singular nouns ending in a consonant other than *-ж*, *-к*, *-х*, *-ц*, *-ч* and *-ш* form the vocative singular by appending *-е*. With very few exceptions, there is no [stress](./Phonology%20(Bulgarian).md#Stress) shift in the vocative singular.
