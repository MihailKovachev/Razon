    Verbs (Bulgarian)#

    Verbs in Bulgarian inflect for person, number, aspect, tense and mood. They have no infinitives. In dictionaries, verbs are listed by their first-person singular present simple form and their conjugation.

    Aspect#

    Every verb in Bulgarian has aspect (вид) and belongs to one of two categories - imperfective aspect (несвършен вид) and perfrective aspect (свършен вид). The vast majority of verbs must thus be learnt in aspectual pairs.

    Imperfective Verbs#

    Imperfective verbs represent actions which are in progress, habitual or repeatedly occuring.

    Most verbs formed via the suffixes ⟨ув⟩, ⟨ов⟩ and ⟨в⟩ without a prefix are imperfective.

    Example: Imperfective Verbs with Suffixes ⟨ув⟩, ⟨ов⟩ and ⟨в⟩

    аресту̀вам to arrest
    атаку̀вам to attack
    гладу̀вам to be hungry
    госту̀вам to be a guest
    дѐйствам to act
    клюка̀рствам to gossip
    критику̀вам to criticize
    лову̀вам to hunt
    нощу̀вам to spend the night
    публику̀вам to make sth public

    Perfective Verbs#

    Perfective verbs глаголи от свършен вид represent actions which are viewed as a complete whole, without reference to duration or repetitiveness.

    Present Forms#

    The present first-person singular serves as the dictionary form of a verb. It is also the only present form which might be completely irregular.

    Building the rest of the present forms requires the present stem which can be obtained in any one of the following ways:

    • the present third-person singular, which is always identical to the present stem;
    • by removing the ending -ш from the present second-person singular;
    • by removing the ending -те from the present second-person plural.

    Dictionaries usually list the present second-person singular to show the present stem.

    Based on the ending of the present stem, verbs are divided into three groups:

    • first conjugation with a present stem in -е;
    • second conjugation with a present stem in -и;
    • third conjugation with a present stem in -а or -я.

    The endings which a verb takes in the present forms are determined by its conjugation group:

    Present Form Endings
    First Conjugation Second Conjugation Third Conjugation
    Singular First Person -а, -я, -м -я, -а -м
    Second Person -ш
    Third Person -
    Plural First Person -м -ме
    Second Person -те

    When stressed, the endings -а, -ат, -я and -ят are pronounced [ɤ], [ɤt], [jɤ] and [jɤt], respectively.

    Building the present forms is done by attaching the appropriate ending to the present stem with a few additional rules depending on the verb's conjugation group. There are no stress shifts, except for irregular first-person singular forms which do not use the entire present stem.

    First-Conjugation Present Forms#

    Verbs of the first conjugation take the following endings in the present forms:

    Present Endings (First Conjugation)
    Singular Plural
    First Person -а, -я, -м -м
    Second Person -ш -те
    Third Person - -ат, -ят

    First-Conjugation Aorist Forms#

    Verbs of the first conjugation are divided into seven subclasses. Some of these subclases form the aorist stem in a highly regular way but some are more invovled.

    The first subclass of conjugation I contains the verbs whose aorist stem is derived from the present stem by replacing the present thematic vowel ⟨е⟩ with the aorist thematic vowel ⟨о⟩ / ⟨е⟩. Specifically, the aorist thematic vowel remains ⟨е⟩ in the second- and third-person singular and is ⟨о⟩ in the rest of the aorist forms.

    A few things to note:

    • These are typically verbs whose present first-person singular ends in -да, -та, -за, -са or -ка with an etymologically non-palatalized ⟨д⟩, ⟨т⟩, ⟨з⟩, ⟨с⟩, ⟨к⟩.
    • In verbs with a present first-person singular ending in -ка, the ⟨к⟩ alternates with ⟨ч⟩ before the aorist thematic vowel ⟨е⟩.
    • If the present stem is stressed on the present thematic vowel, then the aorist stem is stressed on the syllable before the aorist thematic vowel.
    • If the present stem is not stressed on the present thematic vowel, then the aorist stem is stressed on the same place as the present stem.
    • One should beware changes in variable ya.

    The second and third subclasses of conjugation I contain the verbs which satisfy the following conditions simultaneously:

    • The present stem has an etymologycally non-palatalized, non-fricative consonant before the present thematic vowel ⟨е⟩ and this consonant does not undergo alternations when forming the aorist stem.
    • The aorist stem is derived from the present stem by replacing the present thematic vowel ⟨е⟩ with the aorist thematic vowel ⟨а⟩.

    The stress of the aorist stem remains in the same place as the stress of the present stem. For verbs without a prefix as well as for imperfective verbs with a prefix, if the stress of the present stem falls on the syllable directly before the present thematic vowel, then the stress of the aorist stem is allowed to shift to the aorist thematic vowel. Verbs with a present stem ending in -ерѐ drop the ⟨е⟩ before the ⟨р⟩ in the aorist stem.

    Info: Subclass 2 vs Subclass 3

    The sole difference between subclasses 2 and 3 lies in the fact that verbs in subclass 3 have a dictionary form ending in -я: капя, къпя, хапя, etc.

    Second-Conjugation Aorist Forms#

    Verbs of the second conjugation are divided into three subclasses and the formation of their aorist stem is highly regular. The stress of the aorist stem remains in the same place as the stress of the present stem. For verbs without a prefix as well as for imperfective verbs with a prefix, if the stress of the present stem falls on the syllable directly before the present thematic vowel, then the stress of the aorist stem is allowed to shift to the aorist thematic vowel.

    The first subclass of conjugation II contains the verbs whose aorist stem is identical to the present stem.

    Example: Subclass 1 of Conjugation II

    Imperfect Forms#

    The imperfect forms of a verb are constructed by attaching the appropriate ending to the imperfect stem.

    Imperfect Endings
    Singular Plural
    First Person -х -хме
    Second Person -ше -хте
    Third Person -ше -ха

    The imperfect stem is derived in a regular manner from the present stem. This process of formation combined with the fact that the imperfect endings are almost identical to the aorist endings often yields identical imperfect and aorist forms.

    съм ('to be', imperfective)
    Singular Plural Notes
    First Person бях бя̀хме
    Second Person бѐше бя̀хте
    Third Person бѐше бя̀ха
    бъ̀да ('to be', perfective)
    Singular Plural Notes
    First Person бъ̀дех бъ̀дехме
    Second Person бъ̀деше бъ̀дехте
    Third Person бъ̀деше бъ̀деха

    First- and Second-Conjugation Imperfect Forms#

    For verbs of conjugations I and II:

    • If the present stem is not stressed on the present thematic vowel, then the imperfect stem is formed by replacing the present thematic vowel with ⟨е⟩.
    • If the present stem is stressed on the present thematic vowel and the latter is not directly preceded by ⟨ж⟩, ⟨ч⟩ or ⟨ш⟩, then the imperfect stem is formed by replacing the present thematic vowel with ⟨е⟩ (in the second- and third-person singular) / ⟨я⟩ (in the rest of the forms).
    • If the present stem is stressed on the present thematic vowel and the latter is directly preceded by ⟨ж⟩, ⟨ч⟩ or ⟨ш⟩, then the imperfect stem is formed by replacing the present thematic vowel with ⟨е⟩ (in the second- and third-person singular) / ⟨а⟩ (in the rest of the forms).

    The stress of the imperfect stem remains in the same place as that of the present stem.
