# Kinetic Energy

>[!DEFINITION] Definition: Kinetic Energy
>
>The **kinetic energy** of a [point mass](./Inertia.md#Point%20Mass) $m$ is defined as one-half of the product of $m$ and its [speed](./Kinematics.md#Velocity) squared:
>
>$$
>\frac{1}{2}mv^2
>$$
>
>The **(total) kinetic energy** of a [continuous mass distribution](./Inertia.md#Continuous%20Mass%20Distributions) $\rho$ with volume $V$ is the [integral](../../Mathematics/Analysis/Real%20Analysis/Real%20Scalar%20Fields/Integration/Integration%20of%20Real%20Scalar%20Fields.md#Lebesgue%20Integrals)
>
>$$
>\int_V \rho(\boldsymbol{r}) v(\boldsymbol{r})^2 \mathop{\mathrm{d}V},
>$$
>
>where $v(\boldsymbol{r})$ is the [velocity](./Kinematics.md#Velocity) of the infinitesimally small [point mass](./Inertia.md#Point%20Mass) located at $\boldsymbol{r}$.
>
>The **(total) kinetic energy** of a [physical system](./Classical%20Mechanics.md) is the sum of the [kinetic energies](#Kinetic%20Energy) of its components.
>
